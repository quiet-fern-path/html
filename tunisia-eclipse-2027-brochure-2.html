The occasion

A birthday, then the sky goes dark

On Monday 2 August 2027, the Moon's shadow crosses the Tunisian coast. Sfax sits almost exactly on the centreline: nearly six minutes of darkness at mid-morning, the Sun high over the Mediterranean, in the region with the most reliable clear skies anywhere along the eclipse's path. Three days earlier, on 30 July, there is a 30th birthday to celebrate — so the journey is built to do both properly: the classical north first, a slow run south through holy cities and Roman arenas, the birthday by the sea in Sousse, and eclipse day already settled in Sfax with nowhere to rush to.

The setting

Five civilisations deep

Tunisia is not one ancient country but several stacked on top of each other. This route reads them in order:

814 BC

Carthage

Phoenician traders found the city that will rival Rome itself — and lose, twice, before rising again.

146 BC

Rome

Africa Proconsularis becomes the empire's breadbasket, rich enough to build the third-largest amphitheatre on Earth at El Jem.

670 AD

Islam

Kairouan is founded — the fourth-holiest city of Islam, its Great Mosque home to the world's oldest surviving minaret.

1574

Ottomans & Beys

Corsair wealth and Andalusian refugees shape the medinas, palaces and tiled courtyards of Tunis and Sfax.

1956

Modern Tunisia

Independence, and later the birthplace of the Arab Spring — a young republic living inside very old walls.

The journey

Route: Tunis → Sousse → El Jem → Sfax → Paris

North to south down the eclipse's own path, by rail, road and ferry — finishing with a short flight to Paris the morning after totality, and Eurostar home.

Days 1–4 · Sat 24 – Tue 27 Jul

Tunis, Carthage & Sidi Bou Saïd

The capital and its coast: the world's finest Roman mosaics, a living medieval medina, the ruins of Rome's greatest rival, and sunset from a blue-and-white clifftop village.

Café des Nattes, Sidi Bou Saïd. Photo: Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0.

The detail
  • Bardo Museum — the world's largest collection of Roman mosaics, housed in a beylical palace. The ideal first stop: it lays out the whole Punic–Roman–Islamic story before you meet the sites themselves.
  • The medina with Mdinti — themed walks run by a cooperative of the medina's own craftspeople, restaurateurs and booksellers rather than a tour company. Finish in the courtyard of Zitouna Mosque (open to visitors mornings) and on the rooftop of Café Panorama, reached through a carpet shop, for a wraparound view over the old city. Agree drink prices before ordering.
  • Carthage, guided — Byrsa Hill, the Punic ports and the Antonine Baths, with a licensed guide to reassemble a city that was twice erased. Go early; done by midday. Entry to the main sites runs about 12 dinar, all included on one ticket.
  • Sidi Bou Saïd — the cobalt-shuttered village on the cliff above the bay. Time it for golden hour.
  • Dar El Jeld — a splurge dinner in a restored medina mansion with rooftop dining; treat it as the birthday-week dinner.
  • One open day — either a hands-on Tunisian cooking class with a market visit (vegetarian-friendly, English-speaking), or a deliberately empty day at La Marsa's seafront.
Days 4–6 · Tue 27 – Thu 29 Jul

South to Sousse — choose your route

Decision

Every version passes through Kairouan, the holy city, for the Great Mosque at its cool, quiet morning best. The question is what else this leg carries — and it's the one choice that shapes the trip's cost, pace and character. Select an option to see how it changes the budget below.

The Capitol at Dougga — North Africa's best-preserved Roman town. Photo: Kathmandu & Beyond.

What the northwest options actually are
  • Bulla Regia — Roman townhouses built underground to escape the summer heat, their mosaics still in place two storeys down. Unique in the Roman world. Worth a guide at the entrance.
  • Dougga — the best-preserved Roman town in North Africa, on a hilltop of olive groves. Its magic hour is dawn, before the heat and the coaches — which requires overnighting nearby, not just passing through. Entry is a modest 8 dinar.
  • Ain Draham — a mountain town in cork-oak forest at 900m, around 26°C when the coast is past 30°C. The trip's one genuinely cool walk.
  • Makthar — a quiet, little-visited Roman site "surrounded by nature"; features on some ready-made tours as an alternative to the above.
These can't all fit in one leg — a realistic two-day route carries two of them, at most. Dawn at Dougga and Bulla Regia pair well; Ain Draham and Bulla Regia pair well; all three is a squeeze.
Ready-made tours worth looking at for Option C
  • Kairouan, Makthar & Dougga: 12-Hour Roman & Islamic Tour — private, door-to-door, custom itinerary on request. Doesn't include Bulla Regia, and runs Dougga last (afternoon) rather than at dawn as listed — worth asking to reorder.
  • 3-Day Excursions: Kairouan, El Jem, Tunis, Carthage & Dougga — a bundled package of separate day trips based from Tunis, rather than a one-way journey to Sousse. Worth a look if the goal is coverage rather than forward momentum down the coast.
Neither is an exact fit as listed — both are starting points to adapt or use for comparison against a bespoke quote from Didon or Simba.
Days 6–8 · Thu 29 – Sat 31 Jul

Sousse — the birthday

30 July

Two nights in a proper sea-facing hotel, with Friday 30 July kept completely free of travel: a slow birthday of medina, museum, pool and a long dinner.

The Ribat, Sousse medina. Photo: Kathmandu & Beyond.

The detail
  • Sousse Archaeological Museum — the world's second-largest mosaic collection after the Bardo, but intimate: everything here was dug from the city beneath your feet, displayed inside an 11th-century Kasbah with sea views from the ramparts.
  • The Ribat and medina — a UNESCO-listed fortress-monastery and one of Tunisia's liveliest old towns. The medina itself is free to wander; the Ribat is around 8 dinar, the Great Mosque 5 dinar.
  • Optional: Hergla — a small whitewashed fishing village on low cliffs just up the coast; quiet, pretty and refreshingly undeveloped. An easy, unhurried half-day if the mood suits. (Skip Port El Kantaoui, the purpose-built marina resort nearby — it's the opposite of this trip.)
Day 8 · Sat 31 Jul

El Jem, then on to Sfax

Break the southbound train journey at El Jem: the third-largest Roman amphitheatre ever built, rising absurdly out of a small farming town. Walk the gladiators' corridors beneath the arena floor, then continue to Sfax by early evening.

The amphitheatre at El Jem. Entry (incl. the mosaic museum) is around 12 dinar. Photo: Kathmandu & Beyond.

Days 9–11 · Sun 1 – Tue 3 Aug

Sfax — eclipse basecamp

2 August

Tunisia's least touristy major city, and the best-placed city on Earth for this eclipse. A day on the islands, then the main event — watched from Sfax itself, no travel required on the morning.

The detail
  • Kerkennah Islands (Sun 1 Aug) — a 45-minute ferry to low, palm-scattered islands where fishing is still done with palm-frond traps. Hire bikes and ride the flat lanes; long seafood lunch; back by late afternoon. The trip's gentlest day, deliberately placed before the eclipse. Ferries (CTN, from the Jorf terminal) are bought same-day at the dock.
  • Sfax medina — fully walled, almost entirely local: the fish market, the Kasbah, and the Dar Jellouli Museum, a 17th-century merchant's mansion of carved wood and tiled courtyards.
  • Eclipse day (Mon 2 Aug) — partial phases begin mid-morning; totality at roughly 10:08am local, lasting 5m 41s with the Sun 56° up. Sfax sits close enough to the centreline that the city itself — corniche, rooftop or beach promenade — gives essentially the maximum. Afterwards: nothing scheduled. Let it land.
Re-verify the exact contact times with a dedicated eclipse calculator nearer the date, and pack certified eclipse glasses from home.
Days 11–12 · Tue 3 – Wed 4 Aug

Out via Paris

Fly Sfax → Paris Orly the morning after the eclipse — a short local taxi to the airport, no cross-country travel on the busiest travel day Tunisia will have seen in years. An afternoon and evening in Paris, then Eurostar (or a short flight) to London.

Why this routing, and the backup
  • Sfax–Paris (Transavia) is the only European route from Sfax's small airport, flying roughly five times a week — book the moment summer 2027 seats are released, and don't take the earliest departure of the day; give the airport queues margin.
  • Backup: if the flight is sold out or disrupted, the fallback is the train back to Tunis (~4 hours; tickets only sell 3 days ahead, so it can't be pre-booked) and a flight home from Tunis–Carthage. Louages — shared taxis that add cars on demand — are the road alternative if trains are overwhelmed.

Securing it

When to book what

Sfax has around 31 hotels and is about to host a meaningful share of everyone on Earth chasing this eclipse. The order of operations matters more than usual.

Now — Sfax accommodation

Rooms for eclipse week are being released roughly a year out. Check every week or two and book the moment something suitable appears; expect deposits and strict cancellation terms.

≈ 3 August 2026 — London ⇄ Tunis flight opens

British Airways releases bookings exactly 355 days before departure — a fixed rule, not a guideline. For a 24 July 2027 outbound, that's early August 2026: about three weeks from now. Tunisair also flies the route on a similar full-service timeline.

By late Sept/early Oct 2026 at the latest — Sfax → Paris flight

Checked directly with Transavia's own booking policy: summer-season tickets (April–October) go on sale "by the end of September or beginning of October at the latest" the year before — so summer 2027 opens no later than around October 2026. That's a firm upper bound, not the likely date: the wider group has been releasing some routes far earlier this cycle, so it's worth checking every couple of weeks from now. This is the single most oversubscribed booking of the trip — book the day it opens.

Early 2027 — the route decision & remaining hotels

Good to know

Practical notes

Entry & documents
UK citizens need no visa for stays under 90 days — just a passport valid six months beyond the trip. No mandatory vaccinations; hepatitis A, typhoid and an up-to-date tetanus are the standard recommendations.
Heat, and how the itinerary handles it
Early August runs hot: mid-30s °C inland by noon, milder on the coast, with mornings around 23°C. The route is built around it — big sites at opening time, long shaded lunches, sea-facing bases, and the one genuinely cool option (Ain Draham, 900m) available via Options B/C. On eclipse morning the Sun does some of the work: temperatures drop noticeably during totality.
Food & dietary notes
Tunisian cooking is naturally kind to a mostly-vegetarian table: mechouia (grilled vegetable salad), ojja (eggs in spiced tomato), brik au fromage, lablabi (chickpea soup) and vegetable couscous are everywhere and excellent. Fish is the coast's pride — Sfax and Kerkennah especially. For a nut-sensitive traveller the mains are easy territory; pastries are where almonds and other nuts concentrate, so ask there. Alcohol is available but not universal: licensed restaurants and hotels serve wine — look for Tunisian AOC bottles, including the Muscat de Kelibia.
Dress & etiquette
Shoulders and knees covered in medinas and religious sites; Zitouna and Kairouan's Great Mosque admit non-Muslims to courtyards only, mornings. Fridays run to a different rhythm — plan mosque courtyards for other days where possible.
Money & language
The dinar is a closed currency — exchange on arrival, keep receipts, spend down before leaving. Cards work in Tunis, Sousse and bigger hotels; carry cash everywhere else, especially Kairouan, Sfax's medina and Kerkennah. French is the second language everywhere; English-speaking guides are readily bookable (Mdinti, Bardo, Didon Tours all confirmed) but confirm English at every booking rather than assuming.
Getting around
The Tunis–Sousse–Sfax corridor runs on SNCFT trains and comfortable coaches; note train tickets only go on sale three days ahead, so they're bought in-country, not in advance. Louages (shared taxis) fill every gap and scale with demand. The Kerkennah ferry runs from Sfax's Jorf terminal, roughly 8am out and 4pm back, tickets at the dock.
